02 / The problem

Water you can't see, until the damage is done.

India is draining groundwater faster than it refills, and at home the waste stays invisible until the bill arrives.

A nation under water stress

60%

of India's districts are now critical on groundwater. Water is scarce, yet metered like it's endless.

Context / groundwater and usage, India

The drip you never notice

1.4 L/h

is all an overnight leak needs to quietly run up your bill. By the time it shows, it has already cost you.

Illustrative / a typical undetected leak
Where a household's water slips away
Taps and showers left running. A kitchen tap at 1.2 L/min adds up fast.
Overflowing tanks and hoses no one came back to close.
A worn flapper valve refilling a cistern for hours.
Slow pipe leaks behind walls, seen only by the meter.
30 days

The feedback loop is broken.

The old meter reports once a month, in a number you can't act on. No live reading, no alert, no way to stop the flow. Neer closes that gap: from a month of guessing to a glance and a tap.

04 / Industrial design

An object you'd let onto your wall.

A meter only works if it gets installed. Neer is a clean disc that reads as an instrument, not plumbing.

Top view

Form follows the flow it measures.

A low cylinder sits inline on the pipe. Water passes through the sensing chamber and the motorised valve, so one unit reads and stops the flow.

The face is a circle on purpose: a ring is the clearest way to show a quantity filling toward a limit.

05 / HMI design

A face that tells you everything in one look.

The display has one job: make the state of your water clear from across the room. Colour does the talking.

Anatomy / normal state
Figma / device render
Normal / 1.2 L/min

One ring, four meanings.

The ring fills toward the day's limit, and its colour shows how hard the water is running. Tap through the states to watch the face escalate.

Step 01
Detect
Blue ring, live L/min. All normal.
Step 02
Warn
Amber for a minor overflow. Heads up.
Step 03
Shut off
Red alarm, countdown, then the valve closes.
